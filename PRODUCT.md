# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Browser gamers who want to play quick, fun HTML games without waiting for downloads, logins, or bloat. They land on the site for instant gratification — pick a game, play it, move on. The context is casual: killing time, discovering indie experiments, or revisiting arcade nostalgia in a modern wrapper.

## Product Purpose

BrutalGames.online is a neo-brutalist browser arcade — a metadata-driven lobby that hosts standalone HTML games in iframes. The site IS the arcade cabinet row. Success looks like: a visitor lands, immediately sees what's playable, and is playing within seconds. The design should make you feel like you walked into a loud, well-curated arcade — not like you're reading a product page about one.

## Brand Personality

Punchy, irreverent, energetic. The voice is confident and playful — it doesn't explain itself, it just IS the thing. Think arcade marquee energy, not marketing copy. Short declarative statements over paragraph descriptions. The "brutal" in the name comes from the neo-brutalist design language (thick borders, hard shadows, raw color blocks, pixel fonts), not from difficulty or aggression.

## Anti-references

- Polished SaaS landing pages (hero + features + pricing + CTA pattern)
- itch.io's cluttered directory aesthetic (information overload, no visual hierarchy)
- Corporate gaming portals like EA.com (slick, soulless, committee-designed)
- Any "website about games" pattern — this IS the arcade, not a brochure for one

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't describe.** Games are visible and playable instantly. No mission statements, no "how it works" explainers. The arcade speaks for itself.
2. **The page is the machine.** Every element should feel like part of an arcade cabinet — marquees, buttons you want to press, status indicators that glow. Not a webpage with game-themed decoration.
3. **Confidence over explanation.** Short, punchy text. No hedging, no "learn more." If something needs a paragraph to justify its existence, cut it.
4. **Small catalog, big presence.** Two games should feel intentional and curated, not empty and unfinished. Design for the collection you have, not the one you might have someday.
5. **Neo-brutalism is the structure, not the skin.** Thick borders, hard shadows, raw geometry, and unapologetic color are load-bearing architectural decisions, not a theme that could be swapped out.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Maintain what's already in place (skip link, reduced-motion media query, semantic HTML, ARIA labels). Keep contrast accessible. Don't break keyboard navigation. No specific WCAG target beyond general best practice.
